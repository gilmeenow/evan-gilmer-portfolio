MedCASH go-to-market strategy

I built the segmentation, positioning, and launch plan for a health wallet that covers any care cost insurance doesn't. MedCASH launched in October 2026.

The market opportunity & friction

Insurance leaves gaps, and the bills land at the worst time: before payday, before the deductible, every month for chronic care, or on someone else's behalf. One generic message couldn't reach all of those patients, and the team had to decide which buyer to lead with.

Target buyer (ICP)

Employers whose workers face high out-of-pocket costs, followed by health systems.

End users

Four patient segments: cash-flow strained, high co-pay, chronic illness, and caregivers.

Segmentation & tiered packaging

Four patient segments, each mapped to a product tier: Free / Mission, Entry, Premium, and Elite. Each segment gets its own value story instead of one generic pitch.

Launch sequencing

Employers first as the primary B2B buyer, health systems second, with a clustering pilot up front to validate the segments.

Channel flywheel & measurement

The Frank About Health podcast, social, sponsors, and the product each feed the others. UTM attribution flows into KPI dashboards, a GTM tracker, and a RICE-scored roadmap.

Case file · Voice of customer → product

What 68 treatment leaders needed, and the tool built for it

Sources: O'Grady, Lincourt, Gilmer et al., Substance Abuse: Research and Treatment, 2020; O'Grady, Lincourt, Hussain, Gilmer & Neighbors, Journal of Addictive Diseases, 2019.

Product
TPA-8, a progress measure for addiction treatment programs
Audience
Program leaders and counselors; state and health-plan partners
My role
Qualitative coder on the provider study; co-author on the TPA-8 pilot
Core question
As payment shifts to outcomes, what do providers need to prove their value?
Constraints
Providers short on time, staff, and data systems; payers hadn't named the metrics

01Designed for

New York was moving Medicaid addiction treatment toward value-based payment. The state needed to know what providers struggled with and what support would help. Meanwhile, programs lacked a brief tool to show clients were improving, the way the PHQ-9 does for depression care.

02How I did it

One of three coders on five statewide focus groups: a shared codebook in Atlas.ti, 80% agreement before splitting the work, then codes condensed into themes. On the TPA-8 team, insurers and providers shaped the requirements, 16 clients tested the wording, and 393 clients at 10 programs piloted it.

03Outcome

Clear requirements, then a working product. Five themes with recommendations for states and health plans. The TPA-8 produced two reliable scores (α .75) that tracked acuity: outpatient clients scored 6.4 points higher than clients in detox.

What I owned

Coding and synthesis: 345 meaning units condensed into 5 themes

What I contributed to

State recommendations, and the TPA-8 pilot as a co-author

Hero artifact · Key insights

From transcripts to themes

Theme 1Overwhelmed administrators
The TAC is great in having all these webinars that kinda help, but who has time? Sometimes I have it going but I'm doing 20 other things.

Competing demands, thin data infrastructure, staffing gaps, and more acute patients.

Product implicationAnything new has to fit a crowded day. Brevity and low setup cost decide adoption.
Theme 2Confusion and financial fear
No one has come out and said these are the outcomes, prepare yourself, start looking at that, start training your staff.

Unclear metrics, unclear contracting, and doubts that community programs would share in savings.

Product implicationLead with the measure. Buyers commit once they know how success will be counted.
Theme 3Investing in the workforce
When we're developing QI projects it's what do you wanna do? What do you see as something you can work on? Okay great, create a goal around that.

Readiness surveys, training in evidence-based practices, and supports to retain staff.

Product implicationAdoption runs through front-line clinicians. Give them ownership of the goal.
Theme 4Building new business models
We're very data driven… putting in the right questions in the EMR and having people who can pull that information out.

New services, niche populations, new partnerships, and cleaner EHR data.

Product implicationAn early-adopter segment exists. Design for the EHR they already have.
Theme 5Support that fits each program
More individualized consulting opposed to generalized webinars and day-long sessions… each agency is at a totally different point.

Exact metric targets, better health-plan relationships, line-staff training, hands-on help.

Product implicationOne-size onboarding fails here. Segment programs by readiness.

Case file · Adoption research

Only 1 in 6 binge drinkers gets asked. We made asking easy.

Source: O'Grady, Kapoor, Gilmer et al., ACI Open, 2019.

Product
SBIRT for Health Professionals, a free iOS app
Audience
Health coaches, physicians, nurses, and social workers
My role
Usability research: think-aloud sessions, surveys, coding
Core question
Will providers actually use this app during real visits?
Constraints
12 users, all already trained in SBIRT; worry about losing eye contact

01Designed for

Time, training, and stigma keep providers from screening patients for substance use. The app walks any provider from screening to a risk level to a brief intervention tailored to the patient's answers.

02How I did it

I facilitated think-aloud sessions in which 12 health coaches each completed 18 tasks on an iPad, ran the SUS and CSUQ surveys, and coded interviews and audio in Atlas.ti against the FITT framework: how well the person, the task, and the technology fit.

03Outcome

Adoption readiness. 94% first-try task success and a 75.9 usability score, about 10 points above the only other published SBIRT app. Feedback became the revision list.

What I owned

Running the usability study and analyzing the results

What I influenced

The revision list and training plan; the app build belonged to the wider team

Case file · Engagement

Four lifestyle changes at once. One iPad doing the mental math.

Source: Sevick et al., Contemporary Clinical Trials, 2018. ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02276742.

Product
iPad self-monitoring and video group counseling program
Audience
Overweight adults with type 2 diabetes and kidney disease
My role
Operations and technology integration; grant management
Core question
Which digital mix keeps people engaged through four lifestyle changes?
Constraints
Participants new to iPads; strict data security; a 6-month program

01Designed for

People with diabetes and kidney disease must cut calories, sodium, and phosphate additives and move more, all at once. Tracking it is exhausting. The trial tested self-monitoring, theory-based group counseling, or both.

02How I did it

Operations and technology integration at NYU Langone: 4G iPads locked to the study tools (video classes, a food and activity log, dietitian feedback), with no app store or browser, remote wipe, and email behind the NYU firewall.

03Outcome

Engagement. Early cohorts attended a median of 50–62% of sessions and logged meals on about 70% of days, lower than planned. Training was redesigned with screenshot guides, and later cohorts appeared to improve.

What I owned

Trial operations and technology integration

Team outcome I supported

Engagement tracking and the training redesign that followed

GTM methodology

The 3 pillars of evidence-led PMM

Product marketing in health is a behavior-change problem. These are the habits I bring from research into go-to-market.

01

Evidence over opinion

I ground segments and messages in what real users do and say: think-aloud sessions, focus groups, surveys, and market benchmarks, not internal hunches.

68 providers · 12 health coaches · 393 clients
02

Clarity for busy people

Clinicians and patients have seconds, not minutes. One or two questions per screen, eight items at a sixth-grade reading level, one clear promise per segment.

SBIRT app · TPA-8 · MedCASH
03

Measure adoption before scale

Usability, task success, and engagement are leading indicators of adoption. I test them early, then fix what they reveal before spending on reach.

94% task success · 75.9 SUS · ~70% logging days
